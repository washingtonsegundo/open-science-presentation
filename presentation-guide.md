# Complete presentation guide

This guide follows the English-language `main` branch of the presentation. It is intended as a speaking aid rather than a script that must be read verbatim. Each slide has one main message, a short set of points to emphasize, and a suggested transition.

## Slide 1 — Open Science

**Main message:** Brazilian public information infrastructure can support equitable international Open Science cooperation.

**Emphasize:**

- Introduce Washington Segundo, Ibict, and the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation.
- Frame the presentation around public infrastructure, the Global South, and practical cooperation.
- Mention that the QR code links to the complete open repository containing the source and PDF.

**Transition:** “To understand what this cooperation requires, we should begin with the full research cycle.”

## Slide 2 — Open science redesigns the entire research cycle

**Main message:** openness begins with research planning and continues through creation, sharing, reuse, and evaluation.

**Emphasize:**

- Publications, data, code, methods, and reviews are all reusable research outputs.
- Governance, preservation, and participation are dimensions of scientific quality.
- Open Science is a way of designing research, not merely a publication route.

**Transition:** “Reliable openness depends on how information is organized and interpreted.”

## Slide 3 — Information Science is the foundation of reliable AI

**Main message:** trustworthy AI is built on curated, represented, and contextualized information.

**Emphasize:**

- Information Science provides organization, meaning, provenance, and mediation.
- Data Science identifies patterns and models evidence.
- AI can responsibly automate analysis only when the underlying information is reliable.
- Public value is the final purpose of the chain.

**Transition:** “This relationship emerged through a long transformation in scholarly communication.”

## Slide 4 — A long evolution led from private exchange to open ecosystems

**Main message:** Open Science is the latest stage in a centuries-long expansion of scientific communication.

**Emphasize:**

- The movement from private letters to journals created a public scientific record.
- The Web transformed dissemination and separated the traditional functions of journals.
- The Budapest, Bethesda, and Berlin declarations consolidated Open Access.
- The UNESCO Recommendation broadened the agenda from access to an open ecosystem.

**Transition:** “In Brazil, Ibict has helped shape this infrastructure for more than seven decades.”

## Slide 5 — Ibict has shaped scientific information

**Main message:** Ibict combines institutional continuity with a long-standing public mission of openness.

**Emphasize:**

- Ibict was created in 1954 and is a research unit of MCTI.
- Its mission includes organizing, preserving, and disseminating scientific and technological information.
- It develops national services, standards, and interoperable infrastructures.
- Its Open Access and Open Science work predates the current international agenda.

**Transition:** “That history now supports four strategic axes for responding to contemporary information challenges.”

## Slide 6 — Four axes turn information into public value

**Main message:** Information Science connects integrity, citizenship, sovereignty, and openness.

**Emphasize:**

- Information integrity preserves authorship and meaning across the information cycle.
- Informational justice requires equitable conditions for producing, accessing, and using information.
- Information for citizenship includes critical, media, and data literacy, accessibility, and multilingualism.
- Information sovereignty concerns public control of flows, infrastructures, algorithms, standards, software, and hardware.
- Open Science links research practice to trust, cooperation, and science diplomacy.

**Transition:** “These institutional priorities align with a new international framework led by partners from the Global South.”

## Slide 7 — A Global South action plan turns principles into action

**Main message:** the African Union, Brazil, China, and South Africa have proposed a shared pathway from principles to coordinated action.

**Emphasize:**

- The shared principles are equality and mutual benefit, respect for diversity, universal access, and open collaboration.
- The six objectives cover governance, data, infrastructure, scholarly communication, public engagement, and capacity-building.
- The plan supports implementation of the UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science.
- Present the objectives as the organizing framework for the rest of the talk.

**Transition:** “The first implication is that Open Science must be understood as a complete ecosystem.”

## Slide 8 — The ecosystem is broader than open access

**Main message:** sustainable openness connects outputs, processes, infrastructures, and participation.

**Emphasize:**

- Open outputs extend beyond articles to data, software, methods, and educational materials.
- Open processes include preprints, transparent review, responsible assessment, and reproducibility.
- Open infrastructures provide repositories, identifiers, metadata, preservation, and research information systems.
- Open participation includes citizen science, knowledge dialogue, inclusion, and public engagement.

**Transition:** “A broad ecosystem needs principles that balance technical utility with social responsibility.”

## Slide 9 — Four principles balance technical value and social responsibility

**Main message:** FAIR, CARE, TRUST, and DEIA are complementary, not competing, frameworks.

**Emphasize:**

- FAIR enables discovery, access, interoperability, and reuse by people and machines.
- CARE protects collective benefit, authority, responsibility, and ethics.
- TRUST guides transparent, sustainable, user-focused information infrastructures.
- DEIA embeds diversity, equity, inclusion, and accessibility.
- Openness should be effective, strategic, inclusive, and responsible.

**Transition:** “These principles also determine whether open publishing reduces or reproduces inequality.”

## Slide 10 — Open publishing must close the scientific information divide

**Main message:** different Open Access routes distribute costs, control, and exclusion differently.

**Emphasize:**

- Green Open Access relies on repositories and public infrastructure.
- Gold Open Access provides immediate access but may transfer exclusion from readers to authors through fees.
- Diamond Open Access charges neither readers nor authors but requires durable institutional support.
- Journals, repositories, and preprints should expand global circulation without creating new barriers.

**Transition:** “The same public-interest logic is essential when Open Science infrastructure supports AI.”

## Slide 11 — Trustworthy AI begins with trustworthy research infrastructure

**Main message:** AI reliability is a property of the full information system, not only of the model.

**Emphasize:**

- Repositories preserve objects and versions.
- Metadata and persistent identifiers expose context and connect entities.
- Knowledge graphs support meaningful relationships and grounded retrieval.
- Preservation and interoperability enable trusted data spaces and AI-enabled scientific applications under Objective 3.

**Transition:** “Semantic infrastructure is what allows machines to connect meaning rather than only matching words.”

## Slide 12 — Semantic systems connect meaning, not only keywords

**Main message:** grounded retrieval combines semantic representation with curated evidence.

**Emphasize:**

- User questions are translated into semantic representations.
- Controlled vocabularies and ontologies reduce ambiguity.
- Multilingual embeddings connect concepts across languages.
- Retrieval-augmented generation should return answers linked to traceable sources.

**Transition:** “Once that information base is prepared, AI can create practical public value.”

## Slide 13 — Applied AI creates value when the information base is ready

**Main message:** successful AI applications begin with infrastructure and structured data.

**Emphasize:**

- Use ServiçosPI, Patente.IA, RDAPP, and SAVITS as concrete examples.
- Explain the recurring sequence: infrastructure, structured data, then AI application.
- Relate each application to discovery, analysis, monitoring, or evidence-based public policy.
- Stress explainability, curation, and public-interest goals.

**Transition:** “Technical value alone is insufficient if openness does not also address inequality.”

## Slide 14 — Openness must also deliver informational justice

**Main message:** access is only fair when distribution, recognition, and participation are addressed together.

**Emphasize:**

- Distribution concerns equitable access to information and infrastructure.
- Recognition respects diverse peoples, languages, knowledge systems, and epistemologies.
- Participation means meaningful inclusion in producing and governing knowledge.
- Ethical safeguards include consent, privacy, controlled access, collective rights, and protection from harmful use.

**Transition:** “Putting these principles into practice requires institutions and people capable of mediation.”

## Slide 15 — Implementation depends on human mediation

**Main message:** technology and policy only work when multidisciplinary teams can translate them into daily practice.

**Emphasize:**

- Inclusive design responds to different communities, languages, disciplines, and contexts.
- Governance clarifies responsibility, privacy, curation, safeguards, and ownership.
- Digital literacy enables critical and responsible use of data and AI.
- Capacity-building is both a national need and an international cooperation objective.

**Transition:** “Brazil already has significant institutional assets on which this capacity can be built.”

## Slide 16 — Brazil already has the foundations of a national ecosystem

**Main message:** Brazil is not starting from zero; it already has mature platforms, networks, and expertise.

**Emphasize:**

- SciELO and open journals support scholarly communication.
- BDTD and Oasisbr support discovery and access.
- BrCris integrates information about the research system.
- Cariniana supports distributed digital preservation.
- The next step is coordinated governance, stable funding, and stronger interoperability.

**Transition:** “These national capabilities can make concrete contributions to every objective of the international action plan.”

## Slide 17 — Ibict can contribute across all six objectives

**Main message:** Ibict already has services and expertise relevant to all six areas of the plan.

**Emphasize:**

- Governance: policy expertise, networks, standards, and monitoring.
- Open data: Oasisbr, BDTD, aggregation, and metadata interoperability.
- Infrastructure: BrCris, Laguna, Cariniana, dARK, and public digital services.
- Communication: repositories, journals, Open Access, and persistent availability.
- Engagement: information literacy, citizen science, and multilingual access.
- Capacity: professional training, South-South exchange, and open technology transfer.

**Transition:** “Participation in national, regional, and global networks is how these capabilities become shared action.”

## Slide 18 — Networks connect national capacity with global cooperation

**Main message:** networks convert local expertise into standards, mutual learning, and collective influence.

**Emphasize:**

- The Brazilian Digital Repositories Network strengthens national coordination and professional practice.
- The Brazilian Reproducibility Network advances transparent and reproducible research.
- LA Referencia connects national Open Access nodes across Latin America and Spain.
- COAR aligns repository policies, practices, and innovation globally.
- The four network boxes are clickable.

**Transition:** “The next slide shows how Ibict's own services operate as complementary layers.”

## Slide 19 — Ibict connects the national ecosystem in layers

**Main message:** institutional autonomy and national integration can coexist through shared layers and standards.

**Emphasize:**

- Collection and publication remain distributed across institutions.
- Oasisbr and national networks aggregate and connect records.
- BrCris and Laguna support analysis and governance.
- Cariniana and dARK provide preservation and persistent identification.
- The value comes from interoperability across the layers.

**Transition:** “Three infrastructures illustrate how connection, persistence, and semantic integration complement one another.”

## Slide 20 — Three infrastructures strengthen Brazilian research

**Main message:** BrCris, dARK, and Project Laguna solve different but connected infrastructure problems.

**Emphasize:**

- BrCris connects researchers, institutions, projects, funding, and outputs.
- dARK provides persistent, traceable identifiers and long-term access.
- Laguna integrates heterogeneous data through semantic enrichment.
- Together they support governance, reproducibility, discovery, and analysis.

**Transition:** “Project Laguna provides a useful architectural view of this integration process.”

## Slide 21 — Project Laguna architecture

**Main message:** Laguna transforms heterogeneous sources into a reusable semantic and service layer.

**Emphasize:**

- Read the architecture from source formats and harvesting toward integration layers.
- Highlight normalization, semantic enrichment, and the semantic repository.
- Show how APIs, dashboards, and interfaces reuse the integrated base.
- Mention the DOI QR code for the supporting publication.

**Transition:** “BrCris applies this integration logic to build a national view of Brazilian research.”

## Slide 22 — BrCris creates a national view of Brazilian research

**Main message:** BrCris turns fragmented records into a free public gateway to Brazil's research ecosystem.

**Emphasize:**

- BrCris was developed and is led by Ibict.
- It integrates and certifies information from multiple national and international sources.
- The platform supports researchers, managers, funders, journalists, and citizens.
- Explain what the publication count represents and retain its reference date.

**Transition:** “The portal makes this integrated information directly explorable.”

## Slide 23 — BrCris portal

**Main message:** the public interface is the visible result of extensive integration and normalization work.

**Emphasize:**

- Point out search, navigation, data sources, language selection, and dashboards.
- Demonstrate that users can move from aggregated information back to related entities and sources.
- The screenshot and QR code provide direct access to the live service.

**Transition:** “BrCris maps the research system, while Oasisbr focuses on discovering its open digital objects.”

## Slide 24 — Oasisbr connects more than 6.5 million open digital objects

**Main message:** Oasisbr provides national-scale discovery while keeping content distributed at its source institutions.

**Emphasize:**

- More than 6.5 million articles, theses, dissertations, books, chapters, and datasets.
- Approximately 1.2 million documents are aggregated through BDTD.
- Around 2,000 repositories and journals contribute metadata across Brazil.
- Oasisbr connects Brazilian production to national and international discovery networks.
- The QR code links to the service.

**Transition:** “Within this broader aggregation environment, BDTD gives special visibility to graduate research.”

## Slide 25 — BDTD gives visibility to graduate research

**Main message:** theses and dissertations are a strategic part of Brazil's scientific memory and knowledge base.

**Emphasize:**

- BDTD has operated as a national service since 2002.
- It integrates graduate research from Brazilian institutions and records of Brazilians abroad.
- Full texts remain hosted by participating institutions.
- Benefits include visibility, interoperability, specialized knowledge, and accountability for public investment.
- The QR code provides direct access.

**Transition:** “The next comparison clarifies why BDTD, Oasisbr, and BrCris should remain distinct but interoperable.”

## Slide 26 — Oasisbr, BDTD, and BrCris play distinct roles

**Main message:** discovery, specialized graduate knowledge, and research intelligence are complementary functions.

**Emphasize:**

- Oasisbr answers: “What open research outputs can I discover?”
- BDTD answers: “What graduate research has been produced?”
- BrCris answers: “How is the research ecosystem connected?”
- Integration among the services creates more value than a single centralized platform.

**Transition:** “Discovery also depends on identifiers that remain reliable over time.”

## Slide 27 — dARK turns persistence into regional sovereignty

**Main message:** persistent identification is both a technical requirement and a strategic sovereignty capability.

**Emphasize:**

- Decentralization distributes operational responsibility.
- Traceability keeps identifier events auditable.
- Resilience reduces single points of failure.
- Regional institutions retain authority over policies and priorities.
- Explain how dARK works with Oasisbr while access remains with the source institution.

**Transition:** “The following portal view shows the service as an operational infrastructure.”

## Slide 28 — dARK portal

**Main message:** dARK is a working service that can be accessed and integrated, not only a conceptual proposal.

**Emphasize:**

- Briefly identify the public interface and the decentralized ARK approach.
- Use the clickable screenshot or QR code to open the service.
- Note that the visible portal is supported by infrastructure and governance behind the interface.

**Transition:** “Scaling these services internationally requires strong national delivery mechanisms.”

## Slide 29 — National delivery enables international cooperation

**Main message:** equal international participation depends on coordinated and sustainably funded national capacity.

**Emphasize:**

- Establish a focal point connecting domestic and international partners.
- Define roles for government, funders, institutions, and networks.
- Sustain public infrastructures and professional teams.
- Create a permanent exchange mechanism for joint delivery.
- Coordinate resources, pilots, monitoring, reporting, and rotating meetings.

**Transition:** “Cooperation becomes credible when these commitments are measurable.”

## Slide 30 — Measurable commitments make cooperation credible

**Main message:** monitoring should measure interoperability, equity, reuse, and local capability, not only output counts.

**Emphasize:**

- Track platforms connected through open standards.
- Monitor ethical data sharing and benefit safeguards.
- Identify public digital services jointly developed or reused.
- Measure access to papers, preprints, and multilingual outputs.
- Track cross-border training, youth and public engagement, South-South projects, and locally adapted technologies.

**Transition:** “Brazil is well positioned to contribute practical assets to these measurable commitments.”

## Slide 31 — Brazil can help turn the action plan into shared infrastructure

**Main message:** Brazil can move from endorsement to delivery by connecting services, launching pilots, training people, and sharing evidence.

**Emphasize:**

- Trust, collaboration, inclusion, sovereignty, and sustainability are the intended outcomes.
- Ibict offers mature public services and experienced regional partnerships.
- Cooperation should favor equitable, interoperable, and non-commercial infrastructure.
- Propose concrete pilots rather than another layer of declarations.

**Transition:** “The 2027 meetings in Salvador offer a practical milestone for organizing that work.”
